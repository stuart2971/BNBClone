## 5. Getting there

### Airport
**Cancún International (CUN)**, about 20 minutes from Puerto Morelos. Three
terminals: T3 and T4 for major international airlines, T2 for domestic and
international charters.

Route: straight out to Highway 307, then **south 10 miles (15 km)**. There is no
other route. The taxi should bear right/south toward Chetumal, Playa del Carmen
and Tulum. Cancún is north (left); the Hotel Zone is straight ahead.

### Arrival process
Immigration first, then baggage claim, then customs. Free handcarts are available.
Customs checks are random — most people walk through. Porters take dollars or
pesos; tip $1–5 USD depending on the number of bags.

**Timing:** In winter season, arriving Saturday or Sunday means long lines —
clearing immigration and customs can take an hour or more. Weekdays are faster.
**Wednesday is often the best day to fly from the US**, for both price and crowds.

### ⚠️ Warning — the exit corridor
Leaving customs, there are booths with people in official-looking shirts trying to
get your attention. **Ignore them completely and keep walking.** They sell
timeshares and "free" tours that trap you in a sales room for hours.

### Airport transfers — Ride Mexico (preferred partner)
Ride Mexico drivers know all Abbey del Sol property locations and coordinate with
staff to greet arriving guests. Other transport companies often don't know the town.
Contact: ridemexico@gmail.com

**One way (USD):** 1 person $50 · 2–3 people $60 · 4–7 people $70 · 8–10 people $85
**Round trip (USD):** 1 person $90 · 2–3 people $100 · 4–7 people $120 · 8–10 people $140

> **Transportation is NOT included in the vacation rental.**

*(Note: the Helpful Information page separately quotes Ride Mexico at $80 USD
round-trip for 1–3 people. If a guest asks for an exact quote, confirm with the
office rather than guessing.)*

### Taxis
- From the airport: regulated fare **$50–$70 USD one way**, plus tip. There is an
  airport surcharge on departure, which is why leaving costs more than returning.
  Use the official taxi window at the airport.
- Taxis generally **don't know how to find the Abbey del Sol office** — Ride Mexico does.
- **Within Puerto Morelos:** about $2 USD / 35 pesos around town.

### Flights
Skyscanner is recommended for fare research. Monday–Thursday gets the best rates.
Avoid arriving Saturdays — busiest day, longest immigration lines.

### Car rental
Recommendation: **don't rent at the airport.** Get to Puerto Morelos another way,
settle in, get a feel for the town, then rent a car in town. Ask the office for
current in-town rental information.

If renting at CUN: Alamo, National, Dollar, Budget, Executive, Thrifty, plus
cheaper off-site companies. Call the US 800 line to confirm — online reservations
often don't register at the Cancún offices. Bring a printed copy. Pickup can take a
while. **Don't rent a car if you land late at night** — companies frequently run
out, reservation or not.

Liability insurance is **required by Mexican law** and included in every rental.
Agents may claim otherwise and upsell. US insurance generally does not apply in
Mexico — verify with your own carrier.

### Police checkpoints
High police presence when driving. Be a very respectful driver: no speeding, seat
belts on for everyone, no phone use by the driver. If pulled over, ask why. If you
were genuinely in the wrong you'll pay a fine and can't negotiate. They may ask for
your driver's license (don't hand it over) and threaten to impound the car and take
you to the station — this is theatre leading to a request for money.

Don't get angry. Keep large cash out of your wallet. Carry an Abbey del Sol
business card with Jin's number (+52-998-115-2244) and call him if you're held up
for an unreasonable length of time — he can talk to them for you.

## 6. Puerto Morelos — practical local knowledge

### Time zone
Quintana Roo **does not observe daylight saving** and stays on Eastern time all
year. In summer, when the US/Canada are on DST, the town effectively lines up with
**Central time**. In winter it is on **Eastern time**.

### Weather and what to pack
| Period | Conditions |
|---|---|
| Dec – Feb | Warm days ~80°F, cool nights ~60°F, changeable. Cold fronts call for a sweatshirt, jacket, socks. Rain possible but brief. |
| Mar – Apr | Warmer, especially at night |
| May – Jun | Usually hot and humid, tempered by the Caribbean |
| Jul – Aug | Highs high-80s to low-90s, lows high-70s, humid |

Pack light and casual — sandals/flip-flops are the norm. Bring sneakers or walking
shoes for climbing ruins and for cooler evenings. **Bring sunscreen and aloe from
home** — it's expensive in Mexico. The sun is very strong; you'll burn quickly. A
hat is recommended.

### Passport
A **valid passport is required** to enter Mexico, and the expiration date must be
**more than 6 months** from the travel date.

### Electronics through customs
One each of any electronic item (camera, laptop, etc.) per person, for personal use.
**Don't bring anything still in its retail box** — customs will assume it's for
resale and charge duty.

### Money and banking
Two banks in town: **Bancomer** and **CI**. You can exchange dollars there, but the
**ATM rate is better**. Ask the office which ATMs to use.

- ⚠️ **Do NOT use the Cambio (exchange window) by the town square** — they often
  cheat guests and the rate is terrible.
- Use **official bank ATMs**, not free-standing ones, which charge huge fees.
- Carry small change — tipping is expected widely.

### Drinking water
**Never drink tap water anywhere in Mexico.** Brushing your teeth with it is fine.
Each unit gets a 5-gallon bottle of filtered water on arrival, and most properties
supply additional bottles free. Restaurants in the Cancún area use filtered water
for ice and drinking water — the region's livelihood depends on keeping tourists
healthy.

### Groceries and markets
- Grocery/liquor store by the town square, plus convenience stores around town.
- Two large supermarkets on the highway — one is like a small Walmart with
  household goods as well as food.
- **Wednesday morning produce market**, south side of the church on the town
  square, sets up ~7:30 a.m. and runs to about noon. Excellent fruit and vegetables.
  Run by Lupizz Rojas (facebook.com/Frutasyverduraslupizz).
- A truck vendor circulates most days on a loudspeaker selling oranges, mangoes and
  pineapples — usually good and inexpensive.

### Restaurants and nightlife
There's a reference list in the guest information book in each unit, and a
favorites list on the website.

The town is quiet but livens up **Thursday through Sunday**, with 3–4 bars open
until 1 a.m. In winter there are street musicians near the popular restaurants and
events on the town square. Very little spring-break crowd — mostly retirees and
young families. For bigger nightlife, **Playa del Carmen** (about 45 minutes south)
has a 12-block pedestrian-only cobblestone boulevard of restaurants, bars and shops.
Cancún's Hotel Zone is an option for more noise and glitz. Arrange a pickup with the
office.

### Language
About **25% of locals speak English**. For many, Mayan is their first language,
Spanish second, English third. Any attempt at Spanish is appreciated. Abbey del Sol
works with a Spanish language school in town — private lessons in your unit or
group classes at the school. Additional private instructors in winter.

### Bathrooms elsewhere in Mexico
Public bathrooms can be basic — sometimes no toilet seat or paper unless you buy it
from the attendant (2–5 pesos). Carry small change. Reliable bathrooms: gas
stations, big stores (Walmart, Costco), and restaurants — the larger the restaurant
the nicer the bathroom.

### Shopping caution
⚠️ **Avoid the two big, gaudy silver stores on the main corners entering town.**
They are not honest, prices are very high for marginal quality, and guests have been
cheated. Recommended instead: **Godfather Jewelry**, a small store on the right side
of Rojo Gomez.

## 7. Things to do

- **Puerto Morelos beach** — pristine white sand, warm clear water. The **Great Maya
  Reef** is only 1/4 mile offshore, the largest reef system in the Western
  Hemisphere; it shelters the beach from big waves. Beach bars, lounger rental.
- **Diving & snorkeling** — the office recommends dive shops and snorkel operators.
  Options include the reef and calm lagoons.
- **Cenotes** — freshwater sinkholes and caves in the limestone bedrock, cool and
  crystal clear. Unique to this region. The Ruta de los Cenotes runs inland from town.
- **Maya ruins** — the Yucatán has nearly 1,200 archaeological sites, more than
  anywhere else in the Western Hemisphere. Popular: **Tulum**, **Ek Balam**,
  **Chichén Itzá**. The office arranges tours.
- **Jungle adventure parks** — within 30 minutes. Ziplines, ATV jungle rides, cenote
  swims, monkeys and peacocks.
- **Crococun Zoo** — 15 minutes north. Animal refuge and interactive wildlife
  experience.
- **Botanical Garden** — just south of town. Tree-top viewing platform over the
  jungle, monkeys and other wildlife.
- **Islands** — Cozumel (largest, most developed, near Playa del Carmen), Isla
  Mujeres (near Cancún), Holbox (most secluded and natural).
- **Yoga** — Sundara Yoga Shala, one block from the office. Screened-in shala in a
  fenced garden. Instructors Rocio and Patti, all skill levels.
- **Cirque du Soleil "JOYÀ"** — world-class acrobatics, optional dinner, 20 minutes
  south.
- **Bike rentals** — Green Bikes, contact Pilar (facebook.com/PuertoMorelosGreenBikes).
- **Deep-sea fishing**, **sunrise fishing trips**, **Playa del Carmen** day trips,
  **Cancún market** trips.
- **Spanish classes** — Puerto Morelos Spanish Language Center.

### Tours
Abbey del Sol works with several reliable tour operators and recommends **small,
private to semi-private tours** so guests stay flexible with their time. Check in at
the office once settled to go through the options.
